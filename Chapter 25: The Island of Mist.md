# Chapter 25

## The Island of Mist

The evening sun had begun its descent into the western horizon as the five friends stood on the deck of their boat. The golden light of the setting sun bathed the sea in shades of orange and crimson, while the gentle waves reflected the colors of the sky like a vast moving mirror. Behind them lay Mahamathiyapuram, the Great City of Mathi, hidden among forests and ancient ruins. Though they had spent only a short time on the island, it already felt like a place that belonged to another world.

Harish stood quietly near the railing, his eyes fixed on the distant shore. The island seemed peaceful from afar, giving no sign of the mysteries buried beneath its soil. Somewhere within those forests lay forgotten roads, preservation centers, hidden chambers, and the stories of Athiyan and Mathi that had survived for thousands of years.

Joseph adjusted the controls of the boat and slowly guided it away from the island. The familiar sound of the engine filled the air. It was the same boat that had carried them through storms, reefs, and unknown waters, and now it was carrying them home.

Or at least, they hoped so.

Sharan checked the compass and navigation system while Keerthi carefully secured the recovered books and maps in waterproof storage. Abinaya sat near the lantern with the precious artifacts beside her. The Maragatham gemstone rested safely inside its pouch. The mysterious orange gemstone remained wrapped in cloth, while the ancient key and the gold coins bearing Athiyan's seal had been stored with great care.

As the boat sailed farther into the open sea, a strange stillness settled over the waters.

Harish turned back one last time to look at Mahamathiyapuram.

His breath caught.

A pale mist had begun to rise around the island.

At first, no one thought much of it. Sea mist was not uncommon. But within minutes, the fog thickened at an impossible speed. It climbed the cliffs, covered the forests, and slowly swallowed the island itself.

"Joseph anna..." Harish said softly. "Look."

Everyone turned.

The sight before them was unlike anything they had ever seen.

The mist wrapped around Mahamathiyapuram as though it were alive. The ancient island slowly faded behind the white veil. Its hills disappeared first. Then its trees. Then the shoreline itself.

Abinaya quickly unfolded the map and compared it with their direction.

"This cannot be happening," she whispered.

Joseph slowed the boat.

No one spoke.

They simply watched.

Within a few more moments, Mahamathiyapuram vanished completely.

There was no island.

No outline.

No shadow.

Only endless ocean stretched behind them.

Keerthi stared in disbelief. "It was there just now."

Sharan checked the compass again. Everything appeared normal. The coordinates had not changed. The instruments showed nothing unusual.

Yet the island had disappeared.

Joseph leaned against the railing and exhaled slowly. "Now I understand why no one ever found this place."

Abinaya carefully folded the map and held it close. A thought formed in her mind.

"Perhaps Mahamathiyapuram reveals itself only when it wishes to be found."

No one dismissed her words.

After everything they had witnessed, such a possibility no longer seemed impossible.

Night slowly spread across the ocean. The stars emerged one by one in the sky above, while the moon cast its silver glow upon the calm waters. For the first time in many days, the five friends felt safe.

There were no collapsing chambers.

No hidden traps.

No ancient puzzles waiting in the darkness.

Only the sea.

Only the sky.

And the quiet rhythm of waves against the boat.

Keerthi and Harish prepared a simple meal using the remaining supplies and some fish they had stored earlier. Soon the warm smell of food spread across the deck. They sat together beneath the moonlight, sharing stories and laughter that had been absent during their dangerous expedition.

Joseph looked at the horizon with a smile.

"I spent years listening to stories about Kumarigandam," he said. "Never once did I imagine that I would stand upon its land."

Sharan laughed softly. "And now we have proof sitting right beside us."

He glanced toward the waterproof storage compartment.

Inside rested the maps.

The books.

The ancient key.

The royal coins of Athiyan.

Artifacts that could change history itself.

After dinner, the group gathered around the lantern. The sea remained calm as the boat continued its course toward Dhanushkodi. Among all the mysteries they had uncovered, one question remained above all others.

What exactly had Athiyan and Mathi intended for future generations to discover?

Abinaya carefully took out the three ancient books recovered from Mahamathiyapuram. The pages had survived the passage of centuries remarkably well. She gently opened the first book while the others watched closely.

The book described trade routes, ocean currents, and maritime knowledge far ahead of its time. Joseph read some of the passages with amazement.

"Some of these sea routes still exist even today," he said. "Their knowledge of the ocean was extraordinary."

The second book described territories once ruled by Athiyan. Names of lands long swallowed by the sea appeared throughout its pages. Some places sounded familiar from old legends, while others had never been heard before.

Then Abinaya reached for the third book.

Its cover carried an unusual symbol.

A crescent moon surrounded by waves.

The same symbol they had noticed earlier inside the underground chamber.

As she carefully opened its first pages, she noticed something different.

The writing style had changed.

The language felt more personal.

More deliberate.

Her eyes widened slightly.

"What is it?" Harish asked.

Abinaya looked up thoughtfully.

"I think this book was written under Mathi's supervision."

The others leaned closer.

But before she could continue reading, Joseph gently raised his hand.

"We should not rush."

Everyone looked at him.

He smiled.

"We have spent days uncovering mysteries. Let us return home first."

His voice softened.

"Dhanushkodi is where this journey began."

His eyes drifted toward the dark horizon ahead.

"And the priest is waiting there."

The group fell silent.

It was true.

The old priest had guided them from the very beginning. He had recognized the Maragatham gemstone. He had shown them the hidden archives beneath the temple. If anyone could help them understand the books, the gemstones, and the strange disappearance of Mahamathiyapuram, it was him.

Harish looked toward the sea ahead.

Beyond those waters lay Dhanushkodi.

Their home.

Their village.

Their beginning.

Yet they were no longer the same people who had left its shores.

They had touched the lost history of Kumarigandam.

And somewhere within the books resting beside them lay answers that could change everything they knew about the world.

Far away behind them, hidden once more within its veil of mist, Mahamathiyapuram slept beneath the stars.

And ahead of them, Dhanushkodi awaited.

Their journey home had begun.

But deep within the storage compartment, wrapped safely among the artifacts of a forgotten age, the orange gemstone pulsed faintly in the darkness.

As though somewhere beyond the sea, an ancient secret had awakened.

**End of Chapter 25**
