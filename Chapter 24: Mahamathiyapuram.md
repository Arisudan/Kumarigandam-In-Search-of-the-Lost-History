# Chapter 24: Mahamathiyapuram

The five friends stood beneath the open sky, still trying to absorb the discovery they had just made. After spending days beneath the island, walking through forgotten tunnels, solving ancient puzzles, and uncovering the secrets of Athiyan and Mathi, the sunlight felt almost unreal. A cool sea breeze drifted across the coastline, carrying with it the scent of salt and wet earth. Behind them lay the hidden underground city, while before them stretched an island that had suddenly become far more important than any of them had imagined.

Abinaya carefully unfolded one of the maps they had recovered from the Preservation Chamber. The others gathered around her as she spread it across a flat stone. Unlike the other maps they had found, this one appeared to focus entirely on the island itself. Ancient pathways, structures, observation points, and symbols filled the faded parchment. As her eyes moved across the markings, she suddenly stopped. Her finger rested on a line of ancient Tamil text written near the center of the map.

"What is it?" Harish asked, noticing the change in her expression.

Abinaya looked up slowly.

"I think I found the name of this island."

The others immediately leaned closer.

She pointed toward the inscription.

**Mahamathiyapuram.**

For a moment, nobody spoke. The name seemed to carry a strange weight, as though it had been waiting centuries to be spoken again.

Joseph quietly repeated it.

"Mahamathiyapuram..."

"The Great City of Mathi," Abinaya translated.

A silence settled over the group as they absorbed the meaning. Until now, they had always viewed Athiyan as the central figure behind everything they had discovered. He was the king whose symbol appeared throughout the ruins. He was the ruler whose name had survived through legends and inscriptions. Yet this map revealed another side of the story.

The island itself had been named after Mathi.

As Abinaya continued studying the map, more details began to emerge. Symbols scattered across the island marked preservation halls, knowledge centers, archives, and administrative structures. Every location seemed connected through a carefully planned network. It slowly became clear that Mathi had not simply assisted Athiyan in preserving their civilization's history. She had been the architect behind it. The preservation chambers, the hidden records, the maps, and the carefully protected knowledge had all been placed under her supervision.

Keerthi looked up from the map, clearly surprised.

"So Mathi wasn't just helping Athiyan."

Abinaya shook her head.

"No. I think she was leading this entire effort."

Joseph gazed toward the forest stretching across the island. Suddenly everything they had discovered underground made sense. The hidden chambers, the preservation systems, the carefully protected records—none of it could have survived for thousands of years without extraordinary planning. Mathi had created something that was meant to outlive kingdoms, rulers, and even disasters.

For a brief moment, Harish found himself watching Abinaya as she spoke. There was admiration in her voice whenever she mentioned Mathi. He could see how deeply she respected the ancient queen's intelligence and determination. Abinaya noticed him looking and offered a small smile before returning her attention to the map. Harish smiled back without saying a word.

Eventually Joseph folded the map and stood up.

"We should start moving. The boat won't come to us."

The group laughed softly and began preparing for the journey across the island.

The walk was longer than any of them had expected. Dense vegetation covered much of Mahamathiyapuram. Towering trees stretched high above them while thick roots twisted across the ground like natural barriers. Several times they were forced to climb over rocky ridges or push through clusters of bushes. Fortunately, the compass and the ancient map worked surprisingly well together, allowing them to maintain a steady direction toward the opposite shore.

As they crossed the island, evidence of its forgotten past seemed to appear everywhere. Near the base of a hill, Harish discovered carefully shaped stone blocks hidden beneath layers of soil and vines. A little farther ahead, Keerthi found a fallen stone pillar decorated with carvings of ships sailing across the ocean.

The more they walked, the more they realized that Mahamathiyapuram had once been a thriving center of civilization.

At one point, the group came across what appeared to be the remains of an ancient stone road. Although much of it had been swallowed by roots and soil, parts of the pathway still remained visible. The stones had been laid with remarkable precision, surviving centuries of storms, floods, and changing landscapes.

Joseph knelt beside one section and brushed away the dirt.

"Look at this workmanship."

Sharan examined it closely.

"This wasn't built for a small settlement."

Joseph nodded.

"No. This was built for a city."

The realization settled heavily among them. Everything they had seen so far pointed toward the same conclusion. Mahamathiyapuram had once been one of the most important places in Athiyan's territory.

As they continued, the forest slowly began to thin. Sunlight filtered through the trees in golden rays, casting long shadows across the ground. Birds called from the canopy above while the distant sound of waves became louder with every step.

Several kilometers later, Sharan suddenly stopped and pointed ahead.

"Everyone, look."

The others followed his gaze.

Partially hidden among the trees stood the remains of a circular stone structure. Time had destroyed much of it, but the foundation remained intact. Vines wrapped around the ancient stones while moss covered much of the surface.

Abinaya immediately checked the map.

Her eyes widened.

"This place is marked."

"What was it?" Harish asked.

She carefully compared the symbols.

"A preservation center."

The group stared at the ruins with renewed amazement.

Another one.

Not underground.

Not hidden behind puzzles.

Simply forgotten.

It was further proof that Mathi's network had extended across the entire island. The chambers they had discovered beneath the earth were only one part of a much larger system.

The afternoon slowly drifted toward evening as the friends continued their journey. Their bags felt heavier now, not because of the gold coins or the books they carried, but because of what those items represented. Every map, every record, every artifact connected them more deeply to a civilization that history had declared lost forever.

As the hours passed, conversation naturally returned to their discoveries.

"I still can't believe we found the *Kadalkol*," Keerthi said.

"Or the underground city," Sharan added.

Joseph smiled.

"And we're probably still only seeing a small part of the truth."

That thought lingered with them.

If one island could hold so many secrets, how many more remained hidden beneath the Indian Ocean?

How much of Kumarigandam still waited to be discovered?

The questions filled their minds as they walked.

Eventually, the scent of salt became stronger. The sound of crashing waves echoed through the trees.

Joseph grinned.

"We're close."

A few minutes later, the forest opened before them.

The shoreline appeared.

Golden evening light reflected across the ocean's surface, turning the water into a sea of shimmering orange and gold.

And there, floating peacefully where they had left it, was their boat.

For a moment, nobody spoke.

The familiar sight brought an unexpected feeling of comfort.

After everything they had experienced underground, the vessel felt almost like home.

Joseph laughed first.

"I never thought I'd miss a boat this much."

The others laughed with him.

Together they made their way across the beach and finally reached the vessel. One by one, they climbed aboard, carefully securing their bags and equipment.

The maps.

The books.

The copied inscriptions.

The royal gold coins of Athiyan.

Everything had made the journey safely.

As the sun slowly sank toward the horizon, Harish stood on the deck and looked back toward the island.

Somewhere beyond the forests and ruins lay Mahamathiyapuram—the Great City of Mathi.

A city forgotten by the world.

A city that should have vanished beneath the sea long ago.

Yet it still stood.

Waiting.

Protecting its secrets.

And now, for the first time in centuries, those secrets had found new guardians.

The evening wind swept across the ocean as the five friends watched the island disappear into the fading light. None of them knew what answers waited inside the maps and books they had recovered.

But one thing was certain.

The story of Athiyan and Mathi was far from over.

And their greatest discovery still lay ahead.
